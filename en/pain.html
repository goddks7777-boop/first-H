herniated disc

This is a disease in which the disc between the vertebrae protrudes and irritates the nerves. Along with back pain, tingling or loss of sensation in the buttocks and legs may occur.

spinal stenosis

This is a disease in which the spinal canal through which the nerves pass becomes narrowed, causing pain and numbness. Discomfort in the lower back and legs may become worse when standing or walking for long periods of time.

scoliosis

It is a disease in which the alignment of the body changes as the spine bends and rotates sideways. The height of the shoulders or pelvis may be different, or the torso may appear tilted to one side.

sciatica

This is a symptom in which pain extends from the buttocks to the back of the leg when the sciatic nerve is irritated or compressed. Numbness or numbness may also occur.

frozen shoulder

Your shoulder may become stiff and painful, making it difficult to lift your arm or turn it back.

rotator cuff injury

Shoulder tendons may be damaged, causing pain or weakness when lifting your arm.

degenerative arthritis

Pain and stiffness may occur in the joints, which may cause discomfort when walking or going up and down stairs.

tennis elbow

Pain occurs on the outside of the elbow and may become worse when gripping an object or moving the wrist.

Questions to ask before visiting the hospitalCheck before treatment

Q1. Can I receive treatment even if I don’t know the exact name of the disease?

Yes, it’s okay if you don’t know the exact name of the disease. If you let us know where the discomfort is, when the symptoms began, and what movements cause the pain, we will check your condition through treatment.

Q2. Can I bring test results from another hospital?

Yes, please bring any existing test results, video data, and information about the medications you are taking. Current symptoms and existing data are reviewed together for reference in treatment.

Q3. Is Chunana herbal acupuncture performed on all patients?

It is not administered equally to all patients. After checking your symptoms, physical condition, and medical history, we determine the necessity and suitability of treatment, and proceed after a thorough explanation.

Q4. How should I exercise during treatment?

The type and intensity of exercise will depend on your current condition. If you tell us what exercises you usually do, we will guide you through the movements that cause pain and the range that requires adjustment.

Q5. How are the number of treatments and intervals between visits determined?

Plan considering the severity and duration of symptoms and inconveniences in daily life. After treatment, check for changes in pain and movement and adjust the frequency and interval.
